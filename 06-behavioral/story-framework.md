# Story framework: STAR-L

| Part | Share of time | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **S**ituation | 10% | Context in 2–3 sentences: team, system, stakes. Use numbers (users, $ at stake, deadline). |
| **T**ask | 10% | What *you* owned and why it was hard (ambiguity, conflict, scale, risk). |
| **A**ction | 50% | What **you** did: decisions, trade-offs considered, how you influenced others. Say "I", not "we". |
| **R**esult | 20% | Measurable outcome (latency −40%, $X saved, adoption to N teams), plus what happened to people (promotions, ownership). |
| **L**earning | 10% | What you'd do differently; how it changed your approach since. Required for Staff. |

## What makes a story "Staff"

- **Scope:** multiple teams/orgs, multi-quarter, a system others depend on.
- **Ambiguity:** you defined the problem or direction, not just executed a plan.
- **Influence without authority:** you aligned peers or leaders who didn't report to you, using data, prototypes, or written proposals.
- **Technical judgment:** you chose *not* to do something, or you killed your own project when data said so.
- **Multiplying others:** mentoring, raising the bar, creating standards/platforms others adopted.
- **Business impact:** connect the tech to customer or business outcomes.

## Handling follow-ups (where most candidates lose points)

Interviewers dig 2–3 levels deep: "Why that approach? What did you consider? What did your manager think? What would
you do differently? What was the hardest conversation?" Prepare the **second layer** of every story:
the alternatives you rejected, the person who disagreed, the metric you used, the mistake you made.

## Anti-patterns

- "We" everywhere, so the interviewer can't tell what *you* did.
- No conflict, no failure, no trade-off (sounds rehearsed or junior).
- Blaming others; negativity about past employers.
- Stories older than ~5 years unless they're your best example.
- Rambling past 4 minutes before the result.

## Failure stories

Pick a real failure with real consequences, owned by you. Structure: what happened → your part in it → how you fixed or
limited the damage → the systemic change you made so it can't recur → what you learned about yourself.

## Conflict stories

Show you sought to understand the other side, disagreed with data, escalated appropriately (not as a first resort), and
committed fully once a decision was made (Amazon's "Disagree and Commit").
